# Launching Open Communities for DSX AI Factory Projects

Open source is more than public code. A project is open when the people who use it can see how it is run, talk with the people who build it, and help decide what comes next. Bringing the DSX OS projects together in [github.com/dsx-ai-factory](https://github.com/dsx-ai-factory) gave them one home for code. Community meetings give them one home for conversation.

Starting at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026, DSX AI Factory projects are launching their communities: a Slack channel, a mailing list, and open meeting notes and recordings for each project, with public community meetings as each project sets its schedule. Anyone can take part. You do not need to work at NVIDIA, be a customer, or have contributed before.

## Why community meetings

Issues and pull requests work well for specific changes. They work less well for the conversations around them: why a design went one way, what is planned for the next release, or whether a problem you hit is one other people have too. A regular meeting gives those conversations a fixed time and place, and a public record afterwards.

Community meetings are standard practice in open source. Kubernetes special interest groups and many CNCF projects run them, and the DSX AI Factory meetings follow the same model.

## What we hope to learn

- **How the software is used.** Hearing from the people running it in real AI factories, before a design is settled rather than after it ships.
- **What matters most.** Understanding which problems are most important to users, so we can prioritize them.
- **Who wants to contribute.** Giving new contributors a clear way to meet maintainers, find a first issue, and get help.

## What you get

- **A direct line to maintainers.** Ask questions and raise problems with the people who build the project.
- **Visibility into what is coming.** Hear about planned work and releases, and comment on proposals while they can still change.
- **A voice in the roadmap.** Bring your use case. Requests raised in a meeting are filed as GitHub issues so they can be tracked.
- **A place to share.** Demo what you built, or show how you run the software.
- **A record if you miss a meeting.** Notes are public, and recordings of online meetings are shared afterwards.

## How the meetings run

Every project community runs the same way:

- **Open to everyone**, under the project's Code of Conduct.
- **A regular cadence that holds.** Each project picks a schedule and keeps it, even when only a few people attend.
- **A public agenda.** Each project has a meeting notes document that anyone can comment on. Add your topic or question before the meeting.
- **Public notes and recordings.** Notes are taken in the open, and recordings of online meetings go to the project's public recordings folder.
- **Named roles.** A chair hosts the meeting, tech leads speak for the code, a note-taker records it, and moderators keep the meeting and the Slack channel welcoming.
- **Follow-up in the open.** Discussion continues in the project's Slack channel and mailing list, and work is tracked in GitHub issues.

## How to join

1. Request an invite to the DSX AI Factory Slack workspace at [inviter.co/dsx-ai-factory](https://inviter.co/dsx-ai-factory), then join the channel for your project.
2. Join the project's mailing list to receive meeting invites and announcements.
3. Follow the [DSX AI Factory Community calendar](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/embed?src=c_d83d62cba35a7b67c739c701dd77122feefff93389c4cc83b5c2b1d224c07c75%40group.calendar.google.com) for meetings across projects.
4. Come to the next meeting, and bring your questions, your use case, or something you built.

## What comes next

These two communities are the first. Other DSX AI Factory projects are preparing to launch theirs, and each will announce its community in its README and on the calendar when it is ready.
